# Scope and selection policy

## Purpose

The index supports research and discovery across acoustics, audio, musical
instruments, organology, virtual instruments, and closely related technical
fields. It records discoverable source-code repositories and research resources;
it does not rank them and does not assert that every listed repository is active,
complete, open source, or suitable for a particular purpose.

## Core scope

A repository is `core` when its primary purpose has a direct connection to at
least one of the following:

- acoustics as a physical, computational, perceptual, environmental, or engineering discipline;
- musical-instrument acoustics, construction, modelling, classification, or knowledge;
- virtual, digital, augmented, or physically modelled musical instruments;
- acoustic measurement, simulation, auralization, spatial audio, or modal analysis;
- instrument-centred datasets, recognition, transcription, or alignment.

## Adjacent scope

A repository is `adjacent` when it is substantially useful to the core domains
but has a broader or different primary purpose. Examples include general-purpose
synthesis environments, soundscape resources, and broader research datasets
with a clear instrument-acoustics application.

## Normally excluded

- Generic digital-signal-processing or audio I/O infrastructure.
- General music generation without an instrument or acoustics focus.
- Symbolic notation and score-processing tools without relevant acoustic or instrument content.
- Generic speech, source-separation, or audio-AI projects.
- Effects and production tools without a research-relevant acoustics or instrument connection.
- Repositories whose name appears relevant but whose actual subject is outside scope.

Exclusion is a catalogue boundary decision, never a quality judgement.

## Record lifecycle

Availability and activity change over time. Public GitHub metadata is stored as a
dated snapshot. A repository that becomes archived, renamed, or unavailable is
not automatically removed. Stable catalogue identity and historical continuity
take precedence over presenting only currently active projects.
